The Sovereignty Stack

Three properties. All required. None of them optional.

Infrastructure sovereignty is not a feature you can add on. It is a set of architectural properties that either all hold or none of them do.

01

Architecture anonymity

No PII at any layer. Authentication is handle-based — no email, no phone, no government identity. Billing is anonymous by design: cryptocurrency only, no credit card, no bank association. A court order cannot produce what was never collected.

02

Full data ownership

Your workloads run on infrastructure you control, at providers you choose, under credentials you supply. Altostratus is the control plane — it orchestrates, it doesn't host. Your data is not on our infrastructure.

03

No single point of compellability

Infrastructure distributed across multiple providers in multiple jurisdictions means no single legal order reaches all of it. A warrant served on one provider reaches what runs in one jurisdiction — and nothing else.

Why it matters

Privacy by architecture,
not policy.

Policy can be changed under legal pressure. Architecture cannot. Our products are designed to not generate the records most commonly sought in compulsion processes. You cannot subpoena what does not exist.

We use what
we build.

Altostratus runs its own subscription billing on Rudis. If you have ever paid for an Altostratus plan, you have already used Rudis. We do not build privacy tools and then use Stripe.

No sides.
No affiliations.

Privacy is a universal value. Arctic Bison takes no political positions and does not publicly identify clients or engagements. We serve operators who need the tools.

"Most privacy tools protect data in transit or at rest. Very few address the metadata problem: the records that exist not because your data was breached, but because you used the service at all."

— Arctic Bison founding principle
